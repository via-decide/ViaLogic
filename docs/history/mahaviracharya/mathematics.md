## Engineering summary
The mathematical core of Mahāvīrācārya's work is algorithmic arithmetic: operations are presented as repeatable procedures capable of supporting geometry, commerce, series, and algebra.

## Historical summary
The *Gaṇita-sāra-saṅgraha* belongs to the Indian tradition of decimal place-value arithmetic and algebraic problem solving. It is valued because it gathers topics into a mathematics-first treatise.

## Mathematics used
- **Place-value arithmetic:** operations on numbers, powers, roots, fractions, and mixed quantities.
- **Proportional reasoning:** rule-of-three and commercial calculations.
- **Mensuration:** areas, volumes, excavations, and shadow problems.
- **Series:** summation procedures for patterned quantities.
- **Combinatorics:** counting arrangements and selections in rule-based form.
- **Algebraic reasoning:** manipulation of unknown quantities and extraction of roots.

## Theories and procedures
Mahāvīrācārya's mathematics is procedural rather than axiomatic in the Euclidean style. A rule states how to transform input quantities into an answer. In modern terms, many rules are algorithms with preconditions: a particular problem type is recognized, quantities are substituted, arithmetic is performed, and the result is interpreted in the original context.

## Proofs and limitations
The cited historical sources emphasize rules and examples more than formal proofs. That does not make the work unmathematical; it shows a different validation culture. Correctness was demonstrated by trusted rule traditions, worked examples, and practical reproducibility. Some mensuration formulas should be read historically because later mathematics distinguishes exact results from approximations more sharply.

## Influence on future mathematics
By organizing mathematics as a teachable discipline, Mahāvīrācārya helped preserve computational methods for later Indian authors. The long-term influence is visible wherever mathematics is transmitted as operations before it is rebuilt as formal theory.
