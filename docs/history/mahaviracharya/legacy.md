## Engineering summary
Mahāvīrācārya's legacy is the engineering of calculational literacy: define procedures clearly enough that they can be taught, copied, checked, and applied.

## Historical summary
Modern historians value *Gaṇita-sāra-saṅgraha* because it shows mathematics operating as an independent technical discipline in ninth-century India. It is neither merely a biography nor a list of rules; it is evidence of a mature computational culture.

## Computer science
Algorithms are finite procedures. Mahāvīrācārya's rules show the pre-computer form of algorithmic thinking: identify a class of problems and prescribe a sequence of transformations.

## Embedded systems and electronics
There is no direct electronics lineage. The connection is methodological: embedded systems depend on compact procedures that run under constraints, and hand-computation cultures also optimized rules for constrained memory, time, and error checking.

## Operating systems and distributed systems
The relevant legacy is abstraction and documentation rather than direct ancestry. Operating-system manuals and distributed-system protocols rely on unambiguous operations, preconditions, and reproducible examples—the same communication problem solved by mathematical handbooks.

## AI and robotics
AI and robotics rely on numerical methods, geometry, optimization, and state estimation. Mahāvīrācārya did not create these modern fields, but his work belongs to the deeper history of turning quantitative relationships into procedures that can be executed reliably.

## Common misconceptions
- **Misconception:** Mahāvīrācārya is the same figure as Mahavira, the Jain religious teacher. **Correction:** the mathematician is a ninth-century author; the tīrthaṅkara belongs to a much earlier religious history.
- **Misconception:** A mathematical handbook is less important than a theorem. **Correction:** engineering disciplines often advance when procedures become teachable, portable, and standardized.
- **Misconception:** Historical formulas should be judged only by modern notation. **Correction:** their significance lies in the problem classes they made computable for their own users.
